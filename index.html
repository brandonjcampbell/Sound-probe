Setting up
  1. Driver. A phone speaker gives almost nothing below 200 Hz. Use a small contact driver (a bone-conduction or exciter transducer) pressed flat on the surface, fed from this device's headphone or Bluetooth output.
  2. Sensor. A contact microphone on the surface a few centimetres from the driver. A phone resting on it works for a first try.
  3. Reference first. Run the sweep once with the driver and sensor on a plain, non-resonant spot, the same distance apart, and press "Use this run as the reference". Later curves are then shown against it, which removes the driver's and sensor's own colouring.
  4. Same setup each time. Keep the position of everything the same between runs you want to compare.
  5. Keep the volume modest. About the strength of a phone buzzing, no more.
  • Subtracting the direct sound. When the speaker and microphone hear each other directly, set a reference and look at "Reference subtracted": it shows only what changed since the reference. It works only if nothing moves between the two runs.
  • Sweep finds the resonance. The volume figure is A ÷ (peak² − base²), with the two constants below. It only means something for a cavity those constants were fitted to.
  • Pulses give the ring time. With two sensors on the left and right inputs, a known distance apart and in line with the driver, they also give the ripple's speed across the surface.
  • Tone holds one pitch, so the sensor can be moved from spot to spot to find where the note carries.
